# DepRadar Product Principles

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

DepRadar exists to help developers understand their software development journey.

It transforms raw development activity into meaningful stories, replayable sessions, and thoughtful reflections.

The product should make developers feel more aware of how they work, not more anxious about how much they work.

---

# Vision

DepRadar is a Developer Workspace.

It is not an analytics dashboard.

It is not an employee monitoring tool.

It is not a productivity tracker.

It is not an AI wrapper.

DepRadar helps developers:

- Observe
- Understand
- Replay
- Reflect
- Improve

their software development process.

---

# Product Philosophy

Software development is more than commits, pull requests, and lines of code.

It is a sequence of decisions.

A sequence of discoveries.

A sequence of problem-solving moments.

DepRadar exists to preserve and explain those moments.

The product should always tell the story behind the work rather than simply reporting statistics.

---

# Core Values

Every feature inside DepRadar must reinforce at least one of these values.

## 1. Clarity

Users should immediately understand what happened during their development session.

Complexity belongs inside the system, not inside the interface.

---

## 2. Reflection

The product encourages understanding rather than judgement.

Reflection creates awareness.

Judgement creates anxiety.

DepRadar reflects sessions.

It never evaluates developers.

---

## 3. Trust

Everything displayed inside DepRadar must originate from real project data.

The system never fabricates:

- analytics
- timelines
- insights
- AI explanations

Trust is more valuable than impressive visuals.

---

## 4. Explainability

Every insight must be explainable.

If DepRadar presents a conclusion, the user should always be able to understand where that conclusion came from.

Opaque systems reduce trust.

Transparent systems build confidence.

---

## 5. Calm

Developers spend long periods concentrating.

The product should respect that concentration.

Avoid unnecessary interruptions.

Avoid visual noise.

Avoid excessive notifications.

Calm interfaces encourage deeper thinking.

---

## 6. Timelessness

Design decisions should remain relevant for years.

Avoid trends that sacrifice usability.

Choose clarity over novelty.

Choose consistency over fashion.

---

# Product Pillars

The entire product is built upon five pillars.

## Observe

Capture development activity accurately.

---

## Understand

Organize activity into meaningful events.

---

## Replay

Reconstruct development sessions into coherent stories.

---

## Reflect

Help developers understand the structure of their work.

---

## Evolve

Use accumulated understanding to provide better assistance over time.

Future AI features should support this pillar without replacing the previous four.

---

# Design Principles

Every interface should satisfy these principles.

## Story Over Statistics

The product tells the story of development.

Statistics support the story.

They never replace it.

---

## Context Before Detail

Show users what matters first.

Allow exploration later.

Never overwhelm users with information.

---

## Progress Over Perfection

Users should always feel they are moving forward.

Empty states should encourage action.

Errors should explain recovery.

The interface should remain helpful.

---

## Simplicity Through Structure

The product may solve complex problems.

The interface should never appear complicated.

Reduce cognitive load through thoughtful organization.

---

# AI Philosophy

Artificial Intelligence is an enhancement.

It is not the foundation of the product.

The core DepRadar experience must remain useful even if every AI provider is unavailable.

AI should:

- explain
- summarize
- assist
- suggest

AI should never:

- invent facts
- replace deterministic systems
- hide uncertainty
- dominate the interface

The user must always understand when information originates from deterministic analysis versus AI interpretation.

---

# Developer Experience

DepRadar is built for developers.

Every feature should answer at least one question developers naturally ask after coding.

Examples:

- What happened today?
- Where did I spend my time?
- What changed?
- Why did this session feel different?
- What should I revisit tomorrow?

If a feature cannot answer a meaningful developer question, it should be reconsidered.

---

# Long-Term Vision

DepRadar should become a trusted companion throughout the software development lifecycle.

It should support:

- Individual developers
- Students
- Open-source contributors
- Professional engineering teams

without changing its core identity.

As the product grows, every new capability should strengthen the existing experience rather than replacing it.

---

# Decision Framework

Before implementing any feature, ask:

1. Does this help developers understand their work?

2. Does it reduce cognitive load?

3. Is it explainable?

4. Does it strengthen user trust?

5. Does it fit naturally within the existing product?

6. Would removing this feature make the product meaningfully worse?

If the answer to multiple questions is "No", the feature should be redesigned or rejected.

---

# What DepRadar Is Not

DepRadar is NOT:

- an admin dashboard
- an employee surveillance tool
- a productivity scorecard
- a gamified coding platform
- a social network
- a commit counter
- an AI chatbot with developer branding

The product exists to improve understanding, not measure worth.

---

# Product Promise

Every feature added to DepRadar should leave developers with a better understanding of their work than they had before opening the application.

If a feature increases complexity without increasing understanding, it does not belong in DepRadar.

---

# DepRadar Design Manifesto

Every design decision should help developers understand their work more clearly.

If a feature, interaction, animation, insight, or sentence does not improve understanding, it does not belong in DepRadar.
